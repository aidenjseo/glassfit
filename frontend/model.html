How GlassFit works

Everything below runs on your machine. No cloud, no telemetry; camera frames are processed in memory and never written to disk.

1 · Face landmarks

The scan uses Google's MediaPipe FaceLandmarker (the Tasks-API successor to the retired face_mesh solution): a ~3 MB on-device model producing 478 three-dimensional landmarks — the 468-point face mesh plus 10 iris points. One guided scan captures a frontal burst of six frames and two head-turn bursts. Frontal frames are quality-gated (yaw ≤ 12°, pitch ≤ 20°, roll ≤ 15° — calibrated on real portrait photos), rigidly aligned (Procrustes), and reduced to one canonical landmark set by a per-landmark median, which suppresses blinks and jitter.

2 · Millimeters

Landmarks arrive in image coordinates. Your typed pupillary distance anchors the scale: mm per unit = PD / iris-center distance. The head-turn views can't use PD (it foreshortens with rotation), so they self-scale on the near eye's iris ring — the human iris diameter is nearly constant at ≈ 11.7 mm, the same trick MediaPipe Iris uses for depth.

3 · Measurements, honestly labeled

From the canonical set GlassFit computes optician measurements: binocular and monocular PDs, bridge widths at three depths, bridge-crest height, cheekbone and temple widths, face length and jaw width, wrap radius, cheek clearance, canthal tilt, pupil height, and a vertex estimate. The mesh has no ear landmarks, so ear-dependent values are estimates — marked est. in the results — and are upgraded to real geometry (marked side-scan) when the head-turn views captured your ear region. Sides are resolved from the image at runtime, never from index tables.

4 · The rules engine

A pure, versioned rules engine (every tunable lives in a YAML file) maps measurements to a recommendation: frame A/B/DBL/ED/temple, as-worn pantoscopic tilt (kept in the 5–8° band), face-form wrap (5–10°), vertex (12–14 mm), nose-pad setup, temple bends, and per-side raises. Every numeric output records a rule_trace — which rule fired, its inputs, the pre-clamp value — so the learning loop can later compute residuals against it.

5 · Frame matching

Catalog frames pass hard filters first (bridge within ±2 mm, ±3 mm with adjustable pads; temple long enough to reach your ear), then are ranked by a weighted sum of eight explainable components — the breakdown shown under “Why this score”:

ComponentWeightJudges
Optical centration0.20decentration per lens: frame PD (A+DBL) vs your PD
Bridge fit0.22DBL error, asymmetric — narrow pinches; pads soften wide
Width fit0.15frame front vs measured temple width (pinch / slide)
Lens height0.12lens depth below pupil vs measured cheek clearance
Shape affinity0.12face-proportion heuristics (length/width, jaw/cheek, canthal tilt)
Temple fit0.08reach and bend placement
Weight × grip0.06heavy frames on a low bridge slide
Wrap harmony0.05frame wrap vs your as-worn face-form angle

Scores decay smoothly past each tolerance (an exponential tail), so on small or large faces the ranking still discriminates instead of collapsing to ties. The shape thresholds are empirical terciles from 77 real portrait scans, not textbook values — the landmark proxies run wider/taller than classical anthropometry.

6 · The learning loop — how the model was trained

Data. Every scan, recommendation, frame rating, and worn-fit feedback row is stored locally in SQLite with provenance (rules-engine version, landmark model, label author). Ratings snapshot the matcher's own component claims at rating time, so its judgments can be compared against yours even after the algorithm is re-tuned.

Features. The matcher's 8 component scores + fit score, the frame's dimensions/weight and categorical attributes (shape, material, pad type), and your face measurements. Labels: your 1–5 match ratings; worn-fit comfort scores.

Model. Gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting) — chosen for small tabular data with mixed numeric/categorical features and missing values, no tuning required. Evaluation is cross-validated against two no-learning baselines: predicting the mean, and the matcher's own heuristic 1 + 4·fit_score. A model only ships if it beats them.

7 · Validation at scale

The full pipeline was run over 71 public-domain official portraits (119th U.S. Congress, via Wikimedia Commons) plus MediaPipe's reference portraits: 70/71 scanned successfully (the one rejection was a genuinely yawed photo — correct behavior), zero crashes, zero implausible-scale flags, ~40 ms per face. Measured distributions at an assumed 63 mm PD: cheekbone width 141 ± 6 mm, monocular PDs 30.4 ± 2.2 mm — inside adult anthropometric norms. A rotated test image is correctly rejected with coaching.

8 · Privacy

The server binds 127.0.0.1 only. Frames are processed in memory; only derived landmark coordinates and measurements are stored, in one local database file — delete data/runtime/ and every trace of your face is gone.

GlassFit is a fitting aid, not a medical device. Sources: MediaPipe FaceLandmarker (Google, Apache-2.0) · Source Serif 4 (SIL OFL) · catalog and thresholds documented in the repository.
